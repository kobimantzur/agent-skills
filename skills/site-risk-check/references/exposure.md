# Exposure reference

Statutory figures and commonly-reported resolution costs, used to give findings
a sense of scale. **These are reference points, not predictions.**

## Rules for using this file

1. Never compute or present a fine amount *for a specific site*. There is no
   formula from "3 findings" to "you owe $X".
2. Always present the statutory figure and the typical resolution cost side by
   side. Statutory maxima are almost never what anyone actually pays.
3. Always label the total as an illustrative range, never an estimate or a
   prediction.
4. Never cite a figure that is not in this table.
5. If a user asks "what will this cost me", the answer is: nobody can say, and
   the number that matters is the cost of responding, not the statutory maximum.

## Figures

| Regime | Statutory figure | Basis | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CCPA / CPRA** (California) | $2,663 per violation; $7,988 per intentional violation or one involving a consumer under 16 | Per consumer, per violation | Inflation-adjusted for 2025 by the CPPA. The often-quoted $2,500 / $7,500 are the original un-adjusted figures. Enforced by the CPPA and the AG. |
| **CIPA** (California Penal Code §631, §637.2) | $5,000, or three times actual damages, whichever is greater | Private right of action | **Courts disagree** on whether this is per violation or per action. This distinction is the difference between a nuisance claim and a company-ending one. Do not assume per-violation. |
| **VPPA** (federal) | $2,500 per violation | Private right of action | Applies where video-viewing data is disclosed to a third party |
| **GDPR** (EU/EEA) | Up to €20M or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher | Regulator-imposed | Top-of-band figures apply to large-scale infringements; small ecommerce sites are far more likely to face a supervisory-authority order to change practice |
| **ADA Title III** (federal) | No statutory damages; injunctive relief plus plaintiff's legal fees | Private right of action | The cost driver is fees, not damages |

## Typical resolution costs (publicly reported ranges)

What businesses actually pay is settlement and defense cost, not the statutory
maximum. These ranges are widely reported but vary enormously by defendant size,
jurisdiction, and counsel.

| Scenario | Commonly reported range |
|---|---|
| ADA website demand letter, small/mid business, settled pre-suit | $5,000 – $25,000 plus remediation |
| CIPA / pixel demand letter, settled pre-suit | $10,000 – $50,000 |
| Either, litigated rather than settled | six figures, driven by legal fees |
| Regulator inquiry, small business, resolved by changing practice | $0 in penalty, staff time and counsel cost only |

## The number that actually matters

For a small ecommerce business, the realistic exposure is **the cost of
responding to a demand letter**, which is dominated by legal fees regardless of
outcome, plus the remediation you would have to do anyway.

Remediation for the conditions this tool detects is usually a few hours of work.
That asymmetry — hours of work versus five figures of legal fees — is the entire
argument for fixing findings early, and it is a stronger argument than any
statutory maximum.
